# Email patterns

## First email (under 120 words)

```text
Subject: <audience in a few words>, <city>, <month>

Hi <first name>,

<Company> sponsored <similar event> in <year>. <Our event> brings <audience in one line> to <city> on <dates>,
and <one reason the overlap is real: same buyer, same region, a session topic they care about>.

Our <tier> package is <price> and includes <the one benefit that matters most>. <N> of <M> are left.

Worth sending the prospectus?

<Sender name>, <role>, <event>
<event site>
<postal address>
Not the right person, or not interested? Reply "no" and I won't write again.
```

## Follow-up 1 (a week or more later, under 70 words)
One new fact: a confirmed speaker from their buyer's world, a tier selling out, a session they could host. Same ask.

## Follow-up 2 (a week or more after that, under 50 words)
Close the loop: "I'll stop here. If <event> fits a later budget, the prospectus is at <link>." No third follow-up.

## Lines that work
- A dated fact from their record: "You were a Gold sponsor at <event> 2026."
- The room in numbers the organizer can back: "Last year 350 people came, from ticket sales."
- A specific benefit: "a 20-minute session slot", "a table in the main hall", "pre-booked meetings with opted-in attendees".

## Lines to cut
- "I hope this finds you well." "Quick question." "Just following up."
- "Exposure", "visibility", "synergy" with nothing concrete behind them.
- Any claim about why they sponsored elsewhere. You don't know.

## Picking the event to cite
From `get_company_conferences`: prefer `role` sponsor or partner over exhibitor, the same topic over the same city,
and the most recent year. If nothing is similar, lead with the audience instead and skip the "you sponsored" line.
