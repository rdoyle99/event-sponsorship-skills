# Sending rules to check before the user sends

This is a checklist, not legal advice. The sender is responsible; check the rules where the recipient is.

## United States (CAN-SPAM Act; FTC "CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business", ftc.gov)
- Accurate From, To and routing information; a subject line that matches the content.
- A valid physical postal address for the sender.
- A clear way to opt out, honored promptly (the FTC guide gives 10 business days), and no charge or login to opt out.
- The rules apply to business-to-business commercial email too.

## Canada (CASL; crtc.gc.ca)
- Needs consent: express, or implied in set cases (for example an address the person published, with no "no
  unsolicited email" note, and a message relevant to their role).
- Identify the sender and include an unsubscribe mechanism.

## European Union and United Kingdom
- Rules differ by country. Germany requires prior consent for advertising email, business recipients included.
- UK (PECR; ico.org.uk): email to corporate subscribers is allowed without prior consent if the sender is identified
  and an opt-out is given; sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals and need consent.
- Under GDPR, a named person's work email is personal data. Keep a record of where the address came from.

## Always
- One recipient per email, written for that company.
- No purchased contact lists, no scraped addresses, no guessed address patterns.
- Keep an opt-out list and check it before every send.
