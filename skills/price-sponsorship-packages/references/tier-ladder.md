# Building a tier ladder

## Shape
| Tier | Price anchor | How many to sell | What it adds over the tier below |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top (title, Platinum, Presenting) | near the comparables' top package | 1-2 | stage time or naming, first pick of booth space |
| Upper (Gold) | between the middle and the top | 3-5 | a session or a hosted meal, more passes |
| Middle (Silver) | around the comparables' middle | 5-10 | larger booth, lead scans |
| Entry (Bronze, Supporter) | near the comparables' cheapest package | open | logo, a table or small booth, 1-2 passes |

Level medians for reference (ConferenceDB /sponsorship-pricing, read 2026-10-29, US-dollar packages): Platinum
$20,000 (1,021 packages), Gold $10,000 (794), Silver $6,000 (646), Bronze $4,000 (532), booth, table or exhibit space
$3,500 (424). Tier names are not standard across events, so match on what's included, not the name.

## Benefits that justify a step up
- Speaking or stage time (the strongest and the scarcest).
- Meetings: a hosted dinner, a meeting room, pre-booked meetings with attendees who opted in.
- Lead capture: badge scans or the attendee app's lead tool.
- Space: booth size and location.
- Branding: logo placement, lanyards, Wi-Fi name. Weakest on its own.

## Scarcity
Write the count on each tier ("2 available"). Sell the top tier first; it sets the anchor sponsors compare against.

## When comparables are thin
If fewer than 5 comparables publish prices, widen the place (country, then region) before widening the topic, and
say so on the table. Use `similar_events_benchmark` from `get_conference` and quote its `scope` and `events` count.
